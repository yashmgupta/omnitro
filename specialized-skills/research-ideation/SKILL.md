# Biomedical Research Ideation

## Workflow

1. Define the domain, research goal, constraints, available expertise, and intended impact. Separate user-supplied facts from assumptions and request missing context when it would change the ideas.
2. Review verified evidence with the literature-review or biomedical-databases skill when tools and sources are available. If not, mark ideas as unverified and do not imply novelty or claim a literature search.
3. Generate a small set of distinct, testable hypotheses. For each, explain the rationale, prediction, alternative explanation, and the type of evidence that could distinguish them.
4. Compare feasibility, significance, uncertainty, data availability, and ethical or safety considerations. Treat scores as qualitative judgments, not validated measurements; do not overstate translational or clinical readiness.
5. Suggest conceptual next steps and appropriate expert review. Keep experimental suggestions non-operational and avoid requests that enable harmful biological misuse or bypass oversight.

## Deliverable

Give a prioritized idea table with rationale, key uncertainty, safe next step, and evidence links where verified. State where novelty, feasibility, and citations remain to be checked.
