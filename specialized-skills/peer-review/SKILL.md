# Peer Review

## Confidentiality first

Unpublished manuscripts and grants are usually confidential. Before sharing the text with any AI service or third party, confirm that the journal or funder policy allows it. If it does not, stop and tell the user.

## Workflow

1. Read the whole submission and state in two or three sentences what it claims and what evidence it offers. If this summary does not match the authors' intent, that is itself a finding.
2. Evaluate the core questions: Is the question important? Does the design support the conclusions? Are controls, sample sizes, replication units, and blinding adequate? Are statistical methods appropriate, and is multiplicity handled? Do the data support the strength of the language used?
3. Check consistency between abstract, results, figures, tables, and methods, and check that methods are detailed enough to reproduce. Verify that data, code, and materials availability statements are concrete.
4. Check citations for support of the claims they are attached to (see `citation-verification`) and that prior work is fairly represented.
5. Separate major concerns (affect validity of conclusions) from minor concerns (clarity, presentation, typos). For each major concern, explain why it matters and suggest a feasible analysis or experiment that would resolve it.
6. Use specific, evidence-based, and respectful language. Criticize the work, not the authors, and do not demand unrelated new studies.
7. Provide a recommendation consistent with the comments, and keep confidential remarks to the editor separate.

## Deliverable

Summary of the submission, strengths, major concerns, minor concerns, questions to the authors, and a recommendation. Cite page, figure, or line locations. Do not assert fault that you could not verify, and say what you could not assess.
