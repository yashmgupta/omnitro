# Scientific Presentations

## Workflow

1. Fix the constraints: audience expertise, time limit, format (talk, poster, defense), and what the audience should remember. Aim for one main message.
2. Build the narrative as problem, gap, approach, key results, and implications. Include only data that supports the message, and move details to backup slides or supplementary material.
3. Plan roughly one slide per one to two minutes. Give each slide a headline stating its conclusion, for example "Knockout reduces tumor growth by half", not just a topic label.
4. Simplify figures for projection: one idea per figure, large labels, readable fonts, minimal chart junk, direct labels instead of dense legends, consistent colors for the same condition across slides, and colorblind-safe palettes (see `scientific-visualization`).
5. Show uncertainty honestly: sample sizes, error bars with definitions, and statistics where they matter. Do not crop axes or select representative images misleadingly.
6. Credit collaborators, funders, and data sources, and respect permissions for unpublished or third-party material and for patient images.
7. Write speaker notes with transitions and key numbers, rehearse to time, and prepare short answers to likely questions and the weaknesses a skeptic would raise.
8. For posters, lead with the take-home message, use a clear left-to-right or top-to-bottom flow, and keep text minimal.

## Deliverable

Slide-by-slide outline with headlines, figure suggestions, timing, speaker notes, backup slide list, and anticipated questions. Do not add claims not supported by the user's data.
