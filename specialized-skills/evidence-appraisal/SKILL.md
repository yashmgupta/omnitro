# Evidence Appraisal

## Workflow

1. Classify the study design and the question type (therapy, harm, prognosis, diagnosis, mechanism). Appraisal must use criteria suited to the design.
2. Select a recognized tool and name it: RoB 2 (randomized trials), ROBINS-I (non-randomized interventions), QUADAS-2 (diagnostic accuracy), PROBAST (prediction models), AMSTAR 2 (systematic reviews), SYRCLE (animal studies). The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale is common for observational studies but has known limitations.
3. Judge each bias domain from the report: randomization or confounding control, deviations from intended interventions, missing data, outcome measurement, and selective reporting. Quote or cite the passage supporting each judgment. Where the report is silent, record "no information" rather than assuming low risk.
4. For a body of evidence, assess certainty across studies using GRADE domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias, with possible upgrading for large effects, dose-response, or opposing plausible confounding.
5. Consider applicability: population, setting, dose, comparator, and outcome relevance, plus funding and conflicts of interest.
6. Check consistency with other evidence, prior plausibility, and whether prespecified outcomes match those reported (compare against a registered protocol when available).

## Boundaries

A formal rating requires access to the full text and, for GRADE, a judged body of evidence. If only abstracts are available, provide a provisional qualitative appraisal and say so. Do not assign a formal GRADE level without performing the assessment, and do not label a study "high quality" from journal prestige or citation count.

## Deliverable

A domain-by-domain table with supporting evidence and judgment, an overall certainty statement with its basis, key threats to validity, and what additional information would change the assessment.
