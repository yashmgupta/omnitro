# Scientific Writing

## Workflow

1. Identify the document type, audience, intended venue, length, research question, and available evidence. Ask for the actual study methods and findings before writing factual results; use clearly marked placeholders for missing information.
2. Build an outline around the research question and verified evidence. Match claims to supplied results and reliable sources; do not generate fake citations, quotations, study details, sample sizes, or outcomes.
3. For methods, describe what was actually done with sufficient reproducibility and appropriate versions and approvals. For results, report direction, magnitude, uncertainty, sample unit, and relevant comparisons where available.
4. In discussion, distinguish observation, inference, and speculation; address limitations and competing explanations. Avoid causal language for associations unless the design supports it.
5. Apply an appropriate reporting guideline where relevant, such as CONSORT, STROBE, or PRISMA, without claiming compliance unless checked against the actual checklist. Follow journal requirements when supplied.
6. Preserve author intent, disclose substantive edits, and flag unsupported claims or required author verification. Never imply that unverified data or references were checked.

## Deliverable

Produce the requested draft or edit, followed by concise notes on missing evidence and verification needed. Protect confidential material and do not invent ethics approval or informed consent.
