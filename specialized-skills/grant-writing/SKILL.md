# Grant Writing

## Workflow

1. Obtain the funding call, review criteria, page limits, and required sections, and follow them exactly. If the user cannot supply them, state that assumed formats may differ from the actual call.
2. Define the central problem, the gap, and the hypothesis in plain language. A reviewer outside the subfield should be able to say after one page why the work matters.
3. Write aims that are specific, testable, and independent enough that failure of one does not sink the project. Each aim gets a rationale, approach, expected outcome, potential pitfalls, and alternatives.
4. Make rigor explicit: sample size justification (see `statistical-power`), controls, randomization and blinding, authentication of key resources, biological and technical replicates, and planned analyses.
5. Present preliminary data as evidence for feasibility only, with honest limits. Do not overstate or invent results, and mark placeholders as placeholders.
6. Include a realistic timeline, milestones, and risk mitigation, and make the budget narrative consistent with the proposed work and personnel.
7. Address data management and sharing (see `research-data-management`), and ethics, biosafety, and human or animal approvals where relevant (see `research-ethics-biosafety`).
8. Review against the criteria as a skeptical panelist: significance, innovation, approach, investigator and environment. Cut jargon, remove unsupported claims, and tighten to the limit.

## Deliverable

Draft sections mapped to the call's headings, a list of items the user must supply (data, citations, budget figures, institutional details), and a reviewer-style critique. Never fabricate preliminary data, publications, collaborators, or institutional commitments.
