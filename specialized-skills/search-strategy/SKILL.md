# Search Strategy

## Workflow

1. Turn the question into concepts, for example population, intervention or exposure, comparator, and outcome (PICO) or an equivalent framework. Decide inclusion and exclusion criteria before searching.
2. For each concept list synonyms, spelling variants, abbreviations, and controlled vocabulary terms (for example MeSH or Emtree). Use truncation and proximity operators only where the database supports them.
3. Combine synonyms with OR within a concept and concepts with AND. Parenthesize explicitly, because operator precedence differs between interfaces.
4. Choose multiple sources: at least one biomedical database (such as PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase), a multidisciplinary one, trial registries, and preprint servers where relevant. Add citation chasing (references and citing articles) for completeness.
5. Pilot the search against a few known relevant papers. If they are missed, revise the strategy, and watch the trade-off between sensitivity and precision.
6. Record for every database: platform, date searched, exact query string, filters or limits, and number of results. Rerun and update before submission for formal reviews.
7. Deduplicate (see `citation-verification` for DOI-based duplicate detection), then screen titles and abstracts and full texts against the criteria, with a second reviewer for formal reviews, and log reasons for exclusion.
8. Report using an established reporting guideline (see `reporting-guidelines`), including a flow diagram of records.

## Deliverable

Concept table, final search strings per source, search log, screening criteria, and a record count flow. State the limits of the search (language, date range, sources not covered), and do not claim comprehensiveness that was not achieved.
