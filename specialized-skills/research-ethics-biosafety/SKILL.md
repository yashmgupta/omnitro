# Research Ethics and Biosafety

This skill gives conceptual guidance and escalation rules. It is not legal advice and it does not replace institutional review.

## Workflow

1. Classify the work: human participants or identifiable data, animals, biological agents or toxins, recombinant or synthetic nucleic acids, gene drives or germline editing, environmental release, or dual-use potential.
2. Map required oversight: ethics review boards for human research, animal care committees for animal work, institutional biosafety committees for recombinant work and biological agents, and any export, import, or permit rules. State that approval must be obtained before the work, and never advise proceeding without it.
3. For human data, check consent scope against the intended use, minimize identifiers, assess re-identification risk, and respect data use agreements and controlled-access terms. Do not attempt to re-identify individuals.
4. For community or indigenous data, consider community governance and benefit-sharing principles (for example CARE) alongside FAIR.
5. For animal work, apply replacement, reduction, and refinement, and follow reporting guidelines (see `reporting-guidelines`).
6. Dual-use screening: if a request could provide meaningful uplift toward creating or enhancing a dangerous pathogen or toxin, or evading biosecurity controls, decline operational detail and recommend consulting the institutional biosafety office. Discussion of policy, defense, and high-level science remains acceptable.
7. Research integrity: report authorship and contributions honestly, disclose conflicts and funding, avoid fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism, and handle AI assistance transparently per venue policy.
8. When uncertain, escalate to the appropriate committee or officer instead of deciding alone.

## Deliverable

A short ethics and biosafety assessment listing applicable oversight bodies, risks, required approvals, open questions, and recommended escalation. Give no wet-lab protocols for hazardous agents.
