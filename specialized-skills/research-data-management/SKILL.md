# Research Data Management

## Workflow

1. Inventory data types, sizes, sensitivity, and who owns or may access them. Identify consent terms, institutional policy, and funder or journal requirements.
2. Organize with a consistent folder structure and file naming scheme (no spaces, ISO dates, version or processing stage in names). Keep raw data immutable and separate from derived data.
3. Document with a README and a data dictionary: each variable, units, allowed values, missing value codes, and how derived fields were computed. Add provenance (instrument, protocol version, software versions).
4. Use community metadata standards and controlled vocabularies relevant to the field, and persistent identifiers (DOIs, accessions, ORCIDs).
5. Choose the right repository for the data type: domain archives for sequencing, proteomics, structures, or imaging where they exist, and general repositories otherwise. Record accession numbers in the manuscript.
6. Decide access level by sensitivity. Human-subject data may require de-identification, risk assessment for re-identification (genomic data are inherently identifying), and controlled access instead of public release. Follow the consent and approval terms, not just convenience.
7. Choose a license for data and code that matches funder and legal constraints, and state any reuse limits.
8. Back up following a 3-2-1 style pattern, verify restores, and keep checksums. Plan for retention and long-term preservation.
9. Draft the data management and sharing plan and keep it updated as the project evolves.

## Deliverable

A data management plan or checklist with data inventory, structure, metadata, repository and access decisions, license, and responsibilities. Flag anything that needs institutional review rather than deciding it unilaterally.
