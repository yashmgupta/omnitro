# Conceptual Study Design

Use this skill to help frame research questions and compare high-level study designs. This is not a laboratory protocol or clinical care plan.

## Workflow

1. State the question and a testable hypothesis; distinguish exploratory from confirmatory aims.
2. Identify the target population or model, exposure or intervention, comparator, primary outcome, and relevant time frame.
3. Suggest a suitable study-design category and explain why it fits the question. Note alternatives and tradeoffs.
4. Identify potential confounders, biases, inclusion or exclusion considerations, measurement concerns, and likely sources of uncertainty.
5. Recommend that sample-size and power assumptions be determined with appropriate domain expertise and validated inputs. Do not fabricate effect sizes or claim a design is adequately powered without calculation.
6. Flag the need for applicable ethics review, consent, privacy safeguards, biosafety review, and regulatory oversight.

## Boundaries

Keep plans conceptual and non-operational. Do not provide step-by-step wet-lab procedures, optimization parameters, or advice that enables harmful biological misuse or evasion of oversight. When the request requires that level of detail, explain the boundary and offer a safe alternative such as a study-design checklist or a literature summary.

## Output

Present the hypothesis, design, primary outcome, comparison, key validity threats, feasibility questions, and oversight considerations. Label assumptions and items that need expert review.
