# Literature Review

Use this skill when asked to find or synthesize biomedical papers, compare evidence, or identify gaps in a research area.

## Workflow

1. Define the question, relevant population or model, intervention or exposure, comparator, outcomes, and time frame when applicable. Ask only for missing details that would change the search or conclusions.
2. Search available scholarly sources using the tools actually provided. Use a mix of appropriate primary studies, reviews, and authoritative resources; do not imply a comprehensive systematic search unless one was performed.
3. Verify bibliographic details and that each source supports the associated claim. Prefer DOI, PubMed, or publisher links. Never fabricate citations.
4. Extract study type, sample or model, key outcome, result, and major limitations. Distinguish human evidence from animal, cellular, computational, or other preclinical evidence.
5. Compare consistency, disagreement, applicability, and likely sources of bias. Avoid treating association as causation.
6. Summarize evidence strength qualitatively and explain the basis; do not invent a formal certainty rating.

## Output

Give a brief answer first, then an evidence table with source, study type, finding, and limitation where useful. Include source links. Finish with uncertainty and evidence gaps. Clearly disclose when search access was unavailable or limited.
